Learning through making

Explore structure with your hands.

3D-printed and other physical molecular models help students visualize complex DNA and RNA architectures. Drawing structures and reaction mechanisms by hand strengthens spatial reasoning and conceptual understanding.

Teaching projects in development include adapting DNA knot synthesis for undergraduate experiments and creating flexible 3D-printed models that let students manipulate and explore DNA supercoiling.

Research mentorship

Build confidence through discovery.

Structured guidance evolves into independent work. Trainees learn to connect experiments to scientific questions, troubleshoot thoughtfully, evaluate results critically, and share findings in presentations and manuscripts.

Students participate in research through graduate training, undergraduate projects, and Barrett Honors thesis work. Alumni have progressed to faculty positions, PhD programs, and MD/PhD training.

Service & outreach

Share the questions. Open the lab.

Since 2024, Di has hosted six high-school researchers through ASU’s SCience and ENgineering Experience program. Interactive demonstrations and physical models make DNA and RNA structures accessible to younger students and the public.

Community service includes judging at the Arizona Science and Engineering Fair and the 2026 Regeneron International Science and Engineering Fair, alongside proposal review, graduate thesis committees, and university service.
