Di Liu joined Arizona State University in August 2023 as an Assistant Professor in the School of Molecular Sciences and the Biodesign Center for Molecular Design and Biomimetics. His laboratory designs DNA and RNA molecules with controlled structures and properties, connecting fundamental molecular science with biotechnology and human health.

## Education and training

- **Harvard University, 2017–2023:** Postdoctoral research with Prof. Peng Yin at the Wyss Institute and the Department of Systems Biology, Harvard Medical School. Research focused on RNA nanostructures and RNA cryo-EM.
- **University of Chicago, 2011–2016:** Ph.D. in Chemistry (2016) and M.S. in Organic Chemistry (2012), with Prof. Yossi Weizmann. Doctoral thesis: *Synthetic DNA/RNA Topology*.
- **Nanjing University, 2007–2011:** B.S. in Chemistry, with undergraduate research advised by Prof. Zijian Guo.

## Research

Di’s work spans synthetic nucleic acid topology, engineered RNA assemblies, and RNA structure determination. His contributions include DNA four-way junction approaches to complex molecular topologies, RNA branched kissing-loop motifs, and engineered RNA self-assembly for sub-3-Å cryo-EM. He co-developed RhoFold+, a language model-based RNA structure prediction platform reported in *Nature Methods* in 2024.

At ASU, the group is extending these approaches to complex DNA and RNA architectures and chemical strategies for RNA therapeutics. A collaboration with Dr. Eric Tran at Providence Cancer Institute received the 2026 Stand Up To Cancer–Nina Nicolai Pancreatic Cancer Innovation in Collaboration Award.
